# L22 — Troubleshooting Cabling and Physical Issues

## Summary
This is the bottom-to-top half of the [[L21]] methodology: the Layer 1 problems you check first under "question the obvious." There are three groups: **cabling issues** (wrong cable type, signal degradation, bad termination, swapped TX/RX), **interface issues** (speed/duplex mismatch, error counters, port status), and **hardware issues** (PoE power budget, transceivers). The exam gives you a symptom or a `show interface` counter and asks for the cause. Learn the **symptom → cause** pairs.

---

## Core Concepts

### Cabling Issues

#### Incorrect Cable Type

**Fiber: single-mode vs multimode must match the optics** ([[L05]])
| | Single-mode (SMF) | Multimode (MMF) |
|-|------------------|-----------------|
| Core | ~9 µm | 50 or 62.5 µm |
| Light source / wavelength | Laser, 1310/1550 nm | LED/VCSEL, 850/1300 nm |
| Distance | Kilometers | Hundreds of meters |
| Typical jacket color | **Yellow** | **Orange** (OM1/OM2), **aqua** (OM3/OM4), lime (OM5) |

A mismatched SFP and cable means the link won't come up, or it comes up with high errors. **GBICs / SFPs are labeled** with fiber type and wavelength (e.g., `1000BASE-SX` = MMF 850 nm, `1000BASE-LX` = SMF 1310 nm). Colors are a convention, not a guarantee, so check the label.

**Fiber connectors:**
| Connector | Coupling | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| **ST** (straight tip) | **Bayonet** (twist-lock) | Older, mostly multimode; rare in data centers |
| **SC** (subscriber/square connector) | Push-pull | Single fiber per connector |
| **LC** (lucent/little connector) | Push-pull | **Duplex: TX + RX in one connector**. Most common, the SFP standard |
| **MPO/MTP** (multi-fiber push-on) | Push-pull | 12/24 fibers in one ferrule, used for 40/100/400G |

**Copper: the category must support the speed**
| Category | Max Ethernet speed | Note |
|----------|-------------------|------|
| Cat5 | 100 Mbps | **Can't do 1 Gbps reliably**: classic exam trap |
| Cat5e | 1 Gbps | Minimum for gigabit |
| Cat6 | 1 Gbps (10 Gbps ≤ 55 m) | Future-proofing |
| Cat6a | 10 Gbps at 100 m | Current enterprise standard |
| Cat8 | 25/40 Gbps ≤ 30 m | Data center top-of-rack |

**UTP vs STP:** shielded twisted pair resists EMI but must be **grounded** at the termination, or the shield acts as an antenna.

---

#### Signal Degradation

| Problem | Copper | Fiber | Cause | Fix |
|---------|:-----:|:-----:|-------|-----|
| **Crosstalk** | ✔ | ✘ | Signal bleeds from one pair to another. Often caused by **untwisting too much at termination** (NEXT = near-end, FEXT = far-end) | Re-terminate keeping the twists; higher-category cable |
| **EMI / interference** | ✔ | ✘ | Motors, fluorescent ballasts, power lines inducing voltage on the wires | Reroute away from the source, use STP, or **switch to fiber** (immune to EMI) |
| **Attenuation** | ✔ | ✔ | Signal weakens with distance; below the receiver threshold → errors | Respect length limits (**100 m copper**), add a repeater/switch, use SMF for long runs |

**Exam tip:** "The cable runs over the elevator motor / near fluorescent lights" → **EMI**. "The run is 130 m" → **attenuation**. "Errors began after a new jack was installed" → **termination / crosstalk**.

---

#### Improper Termination

**Copper wiring faults** (shown by a cable tester / wire map):
| Fault | What happens | Typical cause |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **Open** | A wire isn't connected end-to-end (a gap) | Wire not seated in the RJ45, cut cable |
| **Short** | Two wires touch each other | Damaged jacket, crushed cable, over-tight bend radius |
| **Cross / crossed pair** | A wire lands on a different pin at the far end | Miswired. **OK only if it's intentionally a crossover cable** |
| **Split pair** | Pin continuity is correct, but wires from **different pairs** are twisted together | Passes a basic continuity test, fails with heavy crosstalk |

**Fiber termination:** an air gap or misalignment between fiber ends, scratched or dirty ferrules. Poor mechanical termination means high insertion loss.

---

#### TX/RX Transposition

Transmit on one end must reach **receive** on the other.
- **Fiber:** duplex LC patch cords can be wired A-to-A instead of A-to-B, giving **no link at all**. Fix: flip the polarity (many LC clips allow swapping the two fibers).
- **Copper:**
  - **Straight-through** (pin 1 → pin 1) is the default.
  - **Crossover** swaps TX/RX pairs (1↔3, 2↔6). It used to be needed for like-to-like device connections (switch–switch, PC–PC).
  - **Auto-MDI-X** on modern NICs and switches detects the wiring and swaps internally, so crossover cables are rarely needed today. **All gigabit (1000BASE-T) ports must support Auto-MDI-X.**

> **Transcript correction:** "auto MDX" is **Auto-MDI-X** (Medium Dependent Interface crossover).

### Interface Issues

#### Speed and Duplex Mismatch

Auto-negotiation should be enabled on **both** sides (or both hard-coded identically).

| Mismatch | Link state | Symptoms |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Speed mismatch** | **Down** (no link). 10/100/1000 use different encodings | Interface down/down, no traffic at all |
| **Duplex mismatch** | **Up**, but performs badly | Slow, especially under load. Error counters climb |

**How a duplex mismatch happens:** One side is hard-coded to `100/full`, the other is set to auto. The auto side can still sense the **speed** from the electrical signal (parallel detection), but it can't learn the duplex, so it **falls back to half duplex** (the standard's rule for 10/100). The result is full on one side and half on the other.

**Counter signature of a duplex mismatch (heavily tested):**
| Side | What you see |
|------|-------------|
| **Half-duplex side** | **Late collisions** (it sees incoming frames while transmitting, after the first 64 bytes, and treats them as collisions) |
| **Full-duplex side** | **CRC/FCS errors and runts** (the half side aborts frames mid-transmission when it detects a "collision") |

> **Transcript clarification:** The lecture says auto-detection fails "without traffic" and falls to the slowest setting. More precisely: parallel detection reads the link signaling, not user traffic, so **speed usually matches but duplex defaults to half**. That's why a duplex mismatch is the common real-world failure, not a speed mismatch.

#### Interface Counters (`show interfaces` on Cisco, `ethtool -S` on Linux)

| Counter | Meaning | Likely cause |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| **CRC / FCS errors** | The receiver's recomputed checksum ≠ the sender's frame check sequence → frame corrupted | **Bad cable / EMI / termination**, or **duplex mismatch** |
| **Runts** | Frames **< 64 bytes** | Duplex mismatch (aborted frames), collisions |
| **Giants** | Frames **> 1518 bytes** (1522 with an 802.1Q tag) | **MTU mismatch**, e.g., jumbo frames (9000) enabled on one side only |
| **Late collisions** | Collision detected **after the first 64 bytes** | **Duplex mismatch** (on the half-duplex side) or cable too long |
| **Drops / discards** | Frames discarded (errors, or queue/buffer full) | Errors above, congestion, QoS policy |

> **Caption note:** **Runts** is a standard counter in Cisco `show interfaces` output (e.g., `show interfaces ethernet 0`: `0 runts, 0 giants, 0 throttles`). The Coursera auto-caption renders it as "runs". That's a transcription artifact, not an error in the lecture.

#### Port Status

| Status | Meaning | Recovery |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **Administratively down** | Shut by command (`shutdown`). **Default on Cisco router interfaces**; switch ports default to up | `no shutdown` |
| **Down/down** (not connected) | No physical link | Check the cable, far-end device, speed mismatch, SFP |
| **Up/down** | Physical link OK, line protocol down | Encapsulation mismatch, keepalives (more WAN/serial) |
| **Error-disabled (err-disabled)** | Switch shut the port **automatically** | `shutdown` → `no shutdown`, **or** `errdisable recovery cause <reason>` + interval for auto-recovery |
| **Suspended** | Port held out of service due to a config inconsistency (e.g., an EtherChannel/LACP member mismatch) | Fix the config to match |

**err-disabled causes:** **port security violation** ([[L20]]), **BPDU guard** (a BPDU on a PortFast port, [[L10]]), **link flapping** (the port goes up/down too often), UDLD, storm control.

```
show interfaces status err-disabled
show errdisable recovery
errdisable recovery cause psecure-violation
errdisable recovery interval 300
```

### Hardware Issues

#### Power over Ethernet (PoE)

**PSE** (power sourcing equipment: a PoE switch or **power injector**) supplies DC power over the Ethernet cable to a **PD** (powered device: phone, AP, camera). The PSE **detects and classifies** the PD before sending full power.

| Standard | Name | Power at PSE port | Power at PD | Pairs used |
|----------|------|------------------|-------------|-----------|
| **802.3af** | PoE | 15.4 W | 12.95 W | 2 |
| **802.3at** | PoE+ | 30 W | 25.5 W | 2 |
| **802.3bt** Type 3 | PoE++ / UPoE | 60 W | 51 W | 4 |
| **802.3bt** Type 4 | PoE++ | 90–100 W | 71.3 W | 4 |

**PoE failure modes:**
| Symptom | Cause |
|---------|-------|
| New AP won't boot or keeps rebooting; older APs fine | **Standard mismatch**: the device needs PoE+ / 802.3bt, but the switch only supplies 802.3af |
| Devices on the highest-numbered ports lose power when a new one is added | **Power budget exceeded**: the switch's total PoE watts are less than the sum of its devices (e.g., 48 ports but a 370 W budget) |
| **Intermittent** reboots/faults under load | Insufficient power. The voltage sags as the draw rises |

```
show power inline            ! Cisco: per-port draw, class, total budget used/remaining
```

**Plan the power budget:** you usually **cannot** power every port at maximum wattage.

#### Transceivers (SFP / SFP+ / QSFP)

| Check | Why |
|-------|-----|
| **Correct type** | Speed (1G SFP vs 10G SFP+), fiber type (SR = MMF, LR = SMF), wavelength must match both ends. Some vendors reject third-party optics (`unsupported transceiver`) |
| **Clean** | **Dust on the ferrule or in the cage** is a top fiber failure cause. Inspect with a fiber scope, clean with a one-click cleaner |
| **Fully seated** | A partially inserted SFP or connector means no link or flapping |
| **Signal strength** | Verify Rx/Tx optical power (dBm) with **DOM/DDM** or a light meter after every fiber connection |

```
show interfaces transceiver detail      ! Cisco DOM: Tx/Rx power in dBm, temperature, thresholds
ethtool -m eth0                          ! Linux: SFP module EEPROM + diagnostics
```
Rx power **below the low-alarm threshold** (e.g., < −14 dBm on a 10G-SR) points to a dirty connector, bad cable, too much distance, or the wrong fiber type.

## Symptom → Cause Cheat Sheet

| Symptom | Most likely cause |
|---------|-------------------|
| No link, new fiber patch | TX/RX transposed, wrong fiber type, dirty connector |
| Gigabit link negotiates at 100 Mbps | Cat5 cable or one bad pair (gigabit needs all 4 pairs) |
| Interface down/down after hard-coding the speed | Speed mismatch |
| Up but slow; late collisions on one side, CRC/runts on the other | **Duplex mismatch** |
| CRC errors, link otherwise fine, near machinery | **EMI** |
| CRC errors on a very long run | **Attenuation** |
| Giants counter climbing | **MTU mismatch** (jumbo frames on one side) |
| Port err-disabled after a user plugged in a small switch | **BPDU guard** or **port security** |
| Port err-disabled repeatedly | **Link flapping** (bad cable or NIC) |
| New AP boot-loops, old phones fine | **PoE standard / budget insufficient** |
| Low Rx dBm on DOM | Dirty optics, bad fiber, wrong type, too long |

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| SMF / MMF | Single-mode (laser, long distance, ~9 µm) / multimode (LED/VCSEL, short, 50/62.5 µm) |
| ST / SC / LC / MPO | Bayonet / push-pull single / push-pull duplex / multi-fiber connectors |
| GBIC / SFP / SFP+ / QSFP | Hot-swappable transceiver modules |
| Crosstalk (NEXT/FEXT) | Signal leaking between wire pairs |
| EMI | Electromagnetic interference from external sources (copper only) |
| Attenuation | Signal loss over distance (copper and fiber) |
| Open / short / crossed / split pair | Copper wiring faults |
| TX/RX transposition | Transmit and receive swapped, so no link |
| Straight-through / crossover | Pin-to-pin / TX-RX swapped cable |
| Auto-MDI-X | Port auto-detects and corrects TX/RX wiring |
| Auto-negotiation | Devices agree on speed and duplex |
| Duplex mismatch | One side full, other half: link up, errors and slowness |
| CRC / FCS | Frame checksum; errors mean corruption |
| Runt / giant | Frame < 64 bytes / > 1518 bytes |
| Late collision | Collision after 64 bytes; duplex mismatch indicator |
| Administratively down | Interface disabled by `shutdown` |
| Err-disabled | Port auto-shut by a violation (port security, BPDU guard, flapping) |
| Link flapping | Interface repeatedly going up/down |
| PoE / PSE / PD | Power over Ethernet / power source / powered device |
| Power budget | Total watts a PSE can deliver across all ports |
| 802.3af / at / bt | PoE (15.4 W) / PoE+ (30 W) / PoE++ (60–100 W) |
| DOM / DDM | Digital optical monitoring: transceiver Tx/Rx power, temperature |

## Real-World Connections

**k3s lab (ARM64 Ubuntu):**
- Check your node NICs right now:
  ```bash
  ethtool eth0                       # Speed, Duplex, Auto-negotiation, Link detected
  ethtool -S eth0 | grep -Ei 'err|crc|drop|collision'
  ip -s link show eth0               # RX/TX errors, dropped, overrun
  ```
  A Raspberry Pi or SBC node stuck at `Speed: 100Mb/s` usually means a bad cable or a pair the cable doesn't carry (some flat "Cat6" cables are junk).
- **MTU mismatch** is the cluster version of "giants": Flannel VXLAN (k3s default) adds 50 bytes of overhead, so the pod MTU is 1450 on a 1500 network. If a node has jumbo frames and another doesn't, large packets silently drop. Small pings work, big transfers (Elasticsearch bulk indexing!) hang. Test: `ping -M do -s 1472 <node>` (1472 + 28 = 1500).
- Ship `node_network_receive_errs_total` (node-exporter) to your monitoring stack and alert on its rate. That's step 6 "preventive measures" from [[L21]] applied to Layer 1.

**AWS/GCP:** No cables, but the same counters exist: EC2 ENA exposes `bw_in_allowance_exceeded`, `pps_allowance_exceeded` (`ethtool -S eth0`), the cloud equivalent of drops from hitting the instance's network limit. MTU: VPCs support 9001 jumbo frames internally, but traffic over the internet/VPN is 1500, which is a classic giant/fragmentation issue on hybrid links.

**Enterprise:** PoE budget is a real design constraint with Wi-Fi 6E/7 APs (802.3bt). DOM thresholds feed SNMP/streaming telemetry ([[L14]]) so dirty optics get caught before links fail.

**Telecom/5G:** Fronthaul links between a radio unit and DU use 10/25G SFP28 optics. eCPRI is extremely sensitive to errors and latency, so DOM monitoring and clean connectors are mission-critical. Same Layer 1 discipline, much tighter tolerance.

## Troubleshooting Scenario

**Scenario:** Users in the accounting office complain the file server is "very slow" since a switch was replaced last night. Pings work. `show interfaces Gi1/0/24` (the uplink to the server's switch) shows:
```
GigabitEthernet1/0/24 is up, line protocol is up
  Full-duplex, 100Mb/s, media type is 10/100/1000BaseTX
  ...
  1243 input errors, 1187 CRC, 0 frame, 0 overrun
  56 runts, 0 giants
  0 late collision
```
The far-end switch port shows **half-duplex, 100 Mb/s** and **2,904 late collisions**.

**Analysis (using [[L21]]):**
1. **Identify:** slow, not down; started after the switch swap (**what changed**).
2. **Theory:** link is up + slow + CRC/runts on one side + late collisions on the other = **duplex mismatch**. The 100 Mb/s on a gigabit port also suggests a hard-coded speed.
3. **Test:** check the running config. The new switch's port was pre-staged with `speed 100` / `duplex full`, while the far end is on auto. The far end detected 100 Mb but fell back to **half**. Confirmed.
4. **Plan:** set both ends to `speed auto` / `duplex auto` (or hard-code both identically) in a maintenance window. Rollback: re-apply the old config.
5. **Implement.**
6. **Verify:** both sides `1000Mb/s Full`, `clear counters`, confirm no new CRC/late collisions, file transfers back to baseline. **Preventive:** update the switch staging template to use auto.
7. **Document** the cause and the template fix.

## Hands-On Ideas

1. **Read your own counters:** `ethtool eth0` and `ethtool -S eth0` on each k3s node. Record speed/duplex/errors as your Layer 1 **baseline**.
2. **Force a duplex mismatch (lab NIC only):** `sudo ethtool -s eth1 speed 100 duplex half autoneg off` on one end of a direct link, leave the other auto, run `iperf3`, and watch the error counters climb. Restore with `autoneg on`.
3. **MTU / giants test:** `ping -M do -s 1472 <peer>` succeeds; `-s 1473` should fail with "message too long". Then check `ip link show flannel.1` for the VXLAN MTU.
4. **Cable tester:** with a cheap wire-map tester, test a deliberately mis-crimped RJ45 (swap two wires) to see a **cross** and a split pair.
5. **PoE math:** pick a switch datasheet (e.g., 48-port, 740 W budget) and calculate how many 802.3bt Type 4 APs vs 802.3af phones it can power at once.

## Quiz

1. A new 10 Gbps fiber link won't come up. The SFP is labeled `10GBASE-LR`, and the patch cable is aqua. What's the most likely issue?
   - A) TX/RX transposed  B) Duplex mismatch  C) PoE budget exceeded  D) Multimode cable used with single-mode optics

2. A workstation's gigabit NIC only links at 100 Mbps. The cable is labeled Cat5. What's the fix?
   - A) Replace it with Cat5e or better  B) Enable Auto-MDI-X  C) Hard-code 1000/full  D) Replace the SFP

3. An interface is up/up but slow. One side shows late collisions; the other shows CRC errors and runts. What's the cause?
   - A) Speed mismatch  B) MTU mismatch  C) Duplex mismatch  D) EMI

4. An engineer hard-codes one side of a link to 1000 Mbps and the other to 100 Mbps. What happens?
   - A) Link is up at 100 Mbps  B) Link is up with late collisions  C) The interface goes down  D) The port becomes err-disabled

5. The giants counter is increasing on a server-facing port. What's most likely?
   - A) Duplex mismatch  B) Jumbo frames / MTU mismatch  C) Attenuation  D) Crosstalk

6. CRC errors appear on a copper run routed alongside a large HVAC motor. What is the problem, and what's the best long-term fix?
   - A) Attenuation; shorter cable  B) Duplex mismatch; set auto  C) Open; re-terminate  D) EMI; reroute the cable or use fiber

7. A cable tester shows pin 2 at one end has no continuity to any pin at the other end. What is this fault?
   - A) Open  B) Short  C) Crossed pair  D) Split pair

8. A user plugged a small unmanaged switch into their wall port, and the port is now err-disabled. Which feature most likely triggered this?
   - A) DHCP snooping  B) BPDU guard or port security  C) Auto-MDI-X  D) PoE classification

9. Which command restores an err-disabled Cisco port manually?
   - A) `clear counters`  B) `shutdown` then `no shutdown`  C) `reload`  D) `speed auto`

10. Six new Wi-Fi 7 APs keep rebooting under load. The older 802.3af VoIP phones on the same switch work fine. What's the most likely cause?
    - A) Duplex mismatch  B) Wrong fiber type  C) The PoE standard or power budget is insufficient for the APs  D) BPDU guard

11. A new duplex LC fiber patch between two switches shows no link. Optics and cable type are correct and clean. What should you check next?
    - A) TX/RX polarity  B) Cable category  C) PoE class  D) VLAN tag

12. Which fiber connector uses a bayonet (twist-lock) coupling?
    - A) LC  B) SC  C) ST  D) MPO

13. A copper run is 140 m between two switches, and the link shows intermittent errors. What's the cause?
    - A) EMI  B) Attenuation, exceeding the 100 m limit  C) Duplex mismatch  D) TX/RX transposition

14. Which tool or feature reports Rx optical power in dBm on a switch SFP?
    - A) Wire map tester  B) TDR  C) `show power inline`  D) DOM/DDM (`show interfaces transceiver`)

**Answers:** 1-D, 2-A, 3-C, 4-C, 5-B, 6-D, 7-A, 8-B, 9-B, 10-C, 11-A, 12-C, 13-B, 14-D
